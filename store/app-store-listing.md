**Description (4000):**
```
SafeHop helps travelers understand which neighborhoods see more reported crime — before you book a hotel, park a rental car or head out for the evening.

Pick a city and the map colors every neighborhood green, yellow or red based on six months of official police data. Tap any area to see what was reported there, how it compares with the rest of the city and how it changed over time.

WHAT YOU CAN SEE
• Pickpocketing & bag/phone snatching
• Car break-ins
• Other theft
• Robbery
• Assault
• Burglary
• Vehicle theft
Filter the map by any category — for example, show only pickpocketing hot spots.

CITIES
Chicago, Los Angeles, New York City, Miami and Seattle.

HOW IT WORKS
SafeHop counts incidents reported to police over the last 6 months and divides them by each area’s size. Areas are compared only with other areas in the same city: green is the third with the fewest reports per km², red the third with the most. Data is refreshed daily as police departments publish new records.

DATA SOURCES
All data comes from official open-data portals:
• Chicago Police Department – data.cityofchicago.org
• Los Angeles Police Department – data.lacity.org
• New York City Police Department – data.cityofnewyork.us
• City of Miami Police Department – City of Miami Open Data
• Seattle Police Department – data.seattle.gov

PLEASE NOTE
SafeHop is an independent app and is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, any police department or government agency. It shows reported incidents, not a guarantee of safety: not every crime is reported, and busy areas see more reports simply because more people are there. New York data is published quarterly. Miami covers the City of Miami only (not Miami Beach). In an emergency, call 911.

PRIVACY
No accounts, no ads, no tracking. SafeHop does not collect personal data or use your location.
```

**Notes:**
```
SafeHop shows aggregated counts of crime incidents reported to police in five U.S. cities (Chicago, Los Angeles, New York City, City of Miami and Seattle), using each police department's official public open-data portal. A daily job aggregates the public records by neighborhood and publishes them as static JSON files; the app downloads those files.

The app has no login, no user-generated content, no ads, and does not collect personal data or location.

How to test: open the app, choose any city, tap a colored area to see its details, and use the category buttons at the top of the map to filter (e.g. "Pickpocketing"). The "About" screen explains the methodology, data sources and limitations.

SafeHop is independent and not affiliated with any government agency.
```
